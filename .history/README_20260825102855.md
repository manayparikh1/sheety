# Sheety
Sheety is basically a browser sheet music web application. You can click piano keys or hum into your microphone, and you can watch the notes appear on a actual staff which plays the tune back based on what you picked. No frameworks and no dependencies and it is just ONE HTML file with around 900 lines of code mixed with css and js.

![](sheety.png)

## Features
 - You can write music by clicking: a 14 key clickable piano spanning from the notes of C4 to B5 which can also be controlled on your computer keyboard.
 - Hum and it gives you notation: you can sing, hum, or whistly and the app detects your pitch in real time and also writes it onto the staff with rests when you pause but you have to go slow and steady because your microphone does not have amazing recognition logic.
 - Real staff rendering: there is treble clef, ledger lines, stems, eigth note, flags, whole notes, automatic bar-lines every 4 beats and finally multi-row wrapping for longer songs.
 - There are key signatures: 11 keys from E flat major E major and symbols get draw next to the clef and every sound transposes automatically to match the scale that gets picked and sharps and flats are included.
 - Playback: there are four instruments, tempo from 40 to a whopping 220 bpm, volume, metronome, and a red highlight that follows the note being played which is really helpful to show what note is going on and when.
 - Save and load to browser storage: there are 3 built in demo tunes, an undo and clear option, and a rests option. The 3 demo tunes include twinkle twinkle, ode to joy, and mary had a little lamb (which is one of my favs lol).
 - Icy theme: I changed the theme like 3 different times and this stuck with me. Frosted glass panels over a layered ice-blue backdrop and css-only falling snow and a new thing I added recently which is a snowflake burst on every click on the keyboard.

## Keyboard
The 14 white keys map to rows of letters that are on your keyboard which are also controllable.

A S D F G H J   →   C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 B4
K L Z X C V B   →   C5 D5 E5 F5 G5 A5 B5

**Space** on your keyboard toggles play and stop and the key repeat is suppressed so holding a letter adds a single not not 50 lol.

### Buttons 

- The play button plays the song from the start.
- The stop buttons stops the plauback and siliences all the notes.
- The rest button adds a rest at the current note length.
- The undo button removes the last note.
- The save/load button stores the song in localstorage under the key *"sheety"*.
- The clear button wipes the song.
- The start humming opens the mic and begins the pitch detection.

### How it works

**Notes are just numbers**
Every note is { i, d } and is a white-key from 0(C4) to 13(B5), and d is one of w/h/q/e for whole, half, quarter, eighth and a rest is i: -1. Thats basically the whole entire data model and the whole song is an array of these.

*The pitch and the poistion of the staff comes from i*

## My Decisions

**Why did I choose key signatures instead of transpositions**

I picked the key signatures as I thought that they were very similar to how the actual sheet music would be. It does not change the notes when the key is changed, but rather the key will tell the application which notes to make sharp and which to make flat. For example, D major makes the F and C sharp.

Moreover, I also decided to go with the standard order of sharps and flats as this made my code much more manageable.

**The Audio**

Every single note is a **OscillatorNode** into a **GainNode**** and it is scheduled ahead of time on the AudioContext. I used methods from **AudioParam** to make the sound change with smoothness and they can be combined into a simple statement which kept the code clean everywhere. I used **setTimeout** because the timing difference does not realy matter.

**Pitch Detection**

I have used the method of autocorrelation to determine the note being hummed by someone. The application correlates the wave with its slightly delayed copy. The amount of delay that goes well with the original wave gives the frequency of the sound, thus giving an idea of the note.

Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and safari are all compatible with this web application because they use **AnalyserNode** and more CSS and JS code blocks which connects to my code meaning that it will run on all these browsers. **The microphone input requires localhost or https**.

## Known Limitations
- Detection pauses in a background tab and it runs on requestAnimationFrame.
- There is only one save slot so saving overwrites whatever was there last.
- The range is fixed at C4-B5 and humming outside that range folds into it rather than textending it.
